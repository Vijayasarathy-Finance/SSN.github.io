How I work

The method behind my case studies, in four steps.

1

Elicit

I start by watching the work and asking people to walk me through it, rather than asking what they want built. I run cross-functional workshops with onshore and offshore stakeholders so differing views surface early, and I record pain points in a list everyone can see. I measure the current state before proposing any change.

2

Prioritize

I score options against criteria agreed before anyone sees the scores, such as time saved, risk, cost and time to value, and I use Pareto analysis to find the few causes behind most of a problem. The fastest option does not always win: in two of my case studies it scored worst on risk and was deferred to a later phase.

3

Resolve conflicts

When two groups want different things, I put both needs side by side, tie them to one measurable goal and agree the decision criteria first. Each activity then gets a single accountable owner in a RACI table, so a disagreement ends in a recorded decision instead of coming back every week.

4

Validate

Every requirement gets an acceptance check in Given, When, Then form and a UAT scenario before build. For riskier changes I prefer a pilot in shadow mode with exit checks agreed in advance, so the old process keeps running while the new one is proven. I also test edge cases, which is how I caught chart-review scenarios that QA had missed.

Healthcare payer know-how

Plain-language notes on the CMS and quality terms I work with, and where I have used them.

Risk Adjustment

Medicare Advantage risk adjustment

CMS adjusts what it pays a Medicare Advantage plan using a risk score for each member, built from the diagnoses reported for that member. Complete, accurate diagnosis data is what makes the payment fair.

Where I used itOwned end-to-end requirements for a Risk Adjustment platform processing CMS regulatory files.

HCC

Hierarchical Condition Category

A group of related ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes used by the CMS risk models. Conditions in the same family form a hierarchy, so only the most severe one counts toward the member’s risk score.

Where I used itDesigned the HCC Combo Code logic: ICD-10-CM combination mapping, UI override mechanics and downstream scoring impact, so coders can resolve complex diagnoses in chart review.

MAO-004

Encounter Data Diagnoses Eligible for Risk Adjustment report

A monthly CMS report that tells a plan which diagnoses from its accepted encounter and chart review records are allowed or disallowed for risk adjustment, so the plan can reconcile what it submitted.

Where I used itOwned requirements for the platform’s handling of MAO-004 and other CMS regulatory files, keeping it aligned with CMS guidelines and client compliance obligations.

HEDIS

Healthcare Effectiveness Data and Information Set

A standard set of clinical quality measures from NCQA that health plans use to report on the care their members receive, such as screenings and chronic condition management.

Where I used itWrote the functional specifications, process flows and user stories for the D3 reporting suite that reports HEDIS Star Ratings to clinical and operations leadership.

Star Ratings

CMS quality ratings for Medicare Advantage plans

CMS rates Medicare Advantage and Part D plans from one to five stars. HEDIS results are one input, alongside member survey scores and other measures. The ratings are public, and higher ones can earn quality bonus payments.

Where I used itIn that suite, Star Ratings sit beside provider and member engagement KPIs, Risk Score revenue projections and chart retrieval metrics.

837, MMR, MOR

The CMS files a risk adjustment platform handles

837 is the standard electronic claim format, in professional and institutional versions. The Monthly Membership Report (MMR) carries each member’s eligibility, risk score and payment. The Model Output Report (MOR) lists the HCCs behind each member’s risk score.

Where I used itOwned the requirements for processing these files, including 837 professional and institutional claims.
