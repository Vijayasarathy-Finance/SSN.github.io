Manufacturing · Quality

Catching Defects Before They Multiply

Cutting units produced before a defect is detected, from ~200 to under 20

~200 → <20units before detection
~$280Kscrap avoided a year
<60 salert latency
The problem

Supervisors learned about defects only after about 200 bad units had been made, locking in scrap, rework and customer risk (about $312K a year in scrap alone).

What I found

“200 units” was really four delays: detection, logging, communication and decision lag. Mapping them showed which to attack first and made the target measurable.

The solution

In-process checkpoints, SPC-based thresholds, tiered alerts with automatic escalation, containment tagging of suspect units, and a response workflow with an audit trail.

Key design decision: treat it as a detection-and-response problem, not a reporting problem. Thresholds are owned by QA, and no alert closes without a recorded root cause.

Talent Acquisition · Hiring

Screening Smarter, Not Harder

Cutting hiring manager screening time without losing good candidates or fairness

40% → 10%manager time on screening
30% → 60%+shortlist precision
~2.4 mopayback, expected case
The problem

Hiring managers spent about 40% of their time on resumes that should never have passed the first filter, and two managers rated the same resume differently.

What I found

About 76% of rejects came from four objective gaps, and three managers agreed on only ~43% of the same resumes. It was a scorecard problem, not a reading-speed problem.

The solution

A signed-off scorecard, hard knockouts only, explainable scoring with recruiter review of every band, 5-8 candidate shortlists on a 48-hour SLA, and fairness monitoring that pauses auto-decline.

Key design decision: assist, don’t decide. Nobody is rejected on score alone. I recommended a rules-based, human-in-the-loop design over AI ranking, using a scored options matrix.
